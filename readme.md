# [Electric Vehicle Optimized Scheduling] Electric Vehicle Optimized Scheduling Based on Model Predictive Control (MPC) with Convex Optimization Algorithm (Matlab Code Implementation)

1. Overview: The electrification of road vehicles has been identified as a key short-term solution to address significant social issues such as climate change and air pollution [1]. Hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), which combine an electric power system with an internal combustion engine, are currently the most common configuration. Despite the low energy density and long charging times of lithium-ion batteries limiting the feasibility of full electric power systems, analysis of daily driving behavior indicates that 50% of the range can be powered by hybrid vehicles equipped with full electric power systems. However, the inclusion of an additional power source introduces a challenging issue: at each moment of the journey, how much power should be provided from the motor and how much from the engine? This is known as the energy management problem [3]. A simple heuristic approach is the charge depletion/charge maintenance strategy, where only electricity is provided from the motor until the battery is fully depleted, then the vehicle operates in a charge maintenance mode until the journey ends [2].
Abstract—This paper provides a detailed study of the computational performance of an algorithm used to solve convex optimization problems related to model predictive control for energy management in hybrid electric vehicles with nonlinear losses. A projection interior-point method is proposed, which reduces the size and complexity of the Newton step matrix inversion by imposing inequality constraints on the control inputs as projections. The properties of the multiplicative algorithm (ADMM) are demonstrated through comparison with the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM). The study finds that the ADMM algorithm has good characteristics when precision is moderate, while the projection interior-point method has advantages when high precision is required. Both methods significantly outperform CVX. The main goal of this paper is to determine the relative computational advantages of second-order and first-order methods for PHEV energy management formulas. The second contribution is a set of numerical studies comparing the performance of the projection interior-point method with the ADMM algorithm from [15]. In these studies, the projection interior-point method demonstrates excellent convergence but requires more time to obtain solutions with medium accuracy, thus being only suitable for real-time solutions within a short range (less than 500 samples in this case). This paper also proves that both methods are much faster than CVX [24] and that the first method can solve long-term energy management problems in real-time (≥1000 samples) when considering nonlinear system dynamics and applying hard constraints on power and charging states throughout the range. The structure of this paper is as follows: Section 2 defines the energy management problem, MPC framework, and convex reconstruction; Section 3 details the projection interior-point method; Section 4 explains the ADMM algorithm from [15]; Section 5 presents numerical experiments; and Section 6 summarizes the paper.
Mathematical models and their explanations are detailed in Part 4.
Running results are presented in Section 3 with Matlab code and detailed explanations available in full text.
